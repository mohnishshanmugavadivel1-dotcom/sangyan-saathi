# Limitations and known failures (v0.4.0-rc2)

Labels: **Established** = reproduced in the development or release-preparation runs; **Unknown** = never measured; **Unverified** = could not be checked.
This file is a curated summary of the development-history limitation list and of the rc2 verification findings. Where an older statement was superseded by rc2, that is said explicitly. Result files cited in the older list (for example `results/s3/...`) are not part of this repository.

## 1. Open safety-relevant misses (do not demo as emergency coverage)
1. **R03 (Established, open):** `I paid the registration amount, then they added a GST charge, then a compliance charge.` returns `ASK_FOLLOWUP` / `USER_PAID` with questions only, no urgent block. A test documents it as an expected failure.
2. **Card details typed after a WhatsApp link (Established):** `I entered my debit card number, expiry and CVV on the link that came in a WhatsApp message.` returns follow-up questions only. (The rc2 credential-entry rule needs wording such as a page or site from an SMS or message; some other phrasings, for example `I entered my UPI PIN on the page the caller sent.`, now escalate. This supersedes an older note that said they did not.)
3. **Hinglish card-detail report (Established):** `maine apna card number aur CVV SMS ke link pe daal diya` is `HIGH_CONCERN` with no urgent block; rc2 has no Hinglish credential-entry rule.
4. **Repeated fees without "I paid" (Established):** `After paying the demat charge, they kept adding new fees every few days.` is not escalated.
5. **Bare code requests (Established):** `Read out the code we send you to confirm your KYC.` is `CANNOT_ASSESS`; a code request is recognised only with a term such as OTP or verification code.
6. **Subject-less, passive or telegraphic reports** create no incident state by design unless rc2's narrow subject-less route applies (a completed payment plus structural risk context).
7. **Selecting a harm situation always escalates**, so the situation choice is the strongest safety control; text-only detection depends on regex rules.

## 2. Over-escalation and friction (Established, mostly not tuned away)
* Ordinary extra-charge payments escalate (`Paid 2,000 advance to the plumber; he asked for 300 more for parts.`, trade-offs B07/B08).
* Past-tense hypotheticals escalate (`If I paid 15,000 would they ask for more?`).
* Any `I will pay ...` text gets a stop-payment step (including bills); P04.
* Ordinary payments get follow-up questions and a red conditional block (`I paid 1,200 to Zomato via UPI`). Checked 2026-10-05: the engine gives this text, an advance-fee story (`I paid the registration amount, then they added a GST charge, then a compliance fee.`) and a look-alike OTP-request text the same state (`USER_PAID`), the same route (`incident_paid_unconfirmed`) and the same four conditional steps, so the block cannot be hidden for the benign one without new detection logic or a vendor-specific rule; it was left unchanged (`test_conditional_urgent_block_stays_for_every_unconfirmed_payment` guards this).
* The Hindi UI text for the bank, 1930, cybercrime-portal, evidence and no-payment steps was read on 2026-10-05 but has **not** been reviewed by a fluent Hindi speaker or professional translator; it is unchanged and should not be described as validated.
* A genuine OTP message (`Enter OTP 482913 to verify your login`) is rated `HIGH_CONCERN`: text cannot tell it from a fake one. Education or warning text containing "guaranteed returns" is flagged.
* The tool never tells a user a message is fine, so genuine messages get no reassurance and users may stop using it.

## 3. Detection limits
* Rules are regexes and fixed word lists; paraphrases outside them are missed (the result then says it could not assess, or asks follow-up questions). Negation uses a short window. Clause splitting is on commas and conjunctions.
* Posture weights are author-chosen and uncalibrated.
* Image or OCR input is not implemented.
* Recovery-scam ("fund recovery agent") guidance is not provided because no corpus source supports it.

## 4. Languages
English is the tested language. Hindi and Hinglish patterns and **all Hindi interface text, labels and guidance were written by the engine's author and not reviewed by a fluent speaker** (including the rc2 label for subject-less payments). Marathi and Tamil have very small keyword lists. Bengali, Telugu, Gujarati and other languages are not read; links and similar signs are still seen and the result says so. The form offers English and Hindi output only. No language is claimed as fully supported.

## 5. Evidence and sources
* **Excerpts removed in this variant:** the result page shows each source's title and link, not what the source says. A user cannot see on the page why a source was matched to a claim, and the claim state (for example "Contradicted by trusted sources") rests on the author's hand-made claim-to-source table, which the user can only check by opening each link. Matching of sources to a message uses titles and topic tags only, so it may differ from the earlier package on messages outside the 392 shipped examples.
* The corpus is a fixed snapshot dated 2026-10-02: 45 source entries on 29 distinct pages (19 official, 0 reproductions, 26 news or other secondary; after the 2026-10-04 source audit below). It does not update itself; a rule change after the snapshot date cannot be detected.
* **Next-step sources changed on 2026-10-04:** the commercial pages LegalBabu and righttoinformation.wiki were removed from the next-steps panel. "Contact your bank" and "stop the payment" now cite the RBI circular of 6 July 2017, which says customers should notify the bank of an unauthorised electronic transaction at the earliest because delay raises the risk of loss; it covers *unauthorised* transactions and does not promise a refund. "Keep these ready" and "keep evidence" cite the MHA/I4C citizen guide on reporting financial cyber fraud, which lists the mobile number, bank or wallet, debited account or UPI ID, transaction ID, date and a screenshot; it does not mention the exact amount, the time, the receiving account, chat history or phone numbers (the steps say so), and the document is headed "For Delhi Only" (nationwide scope unverified). SEBI and RBI do not take general cybercrime reports and no step says they do; 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in remain the reporting channel. LegalBabu (`LEGALBABU-NOGUARANTEE`, claim evidence only) remains in the source list.
* **Source audit of 2026-10-04 (next-steps panel):** (a) SEBI PR 14/2026 entries now point at SEBI's own PDF (sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/feb-2026/1772113926582.pdf, fetched 2026-10-04) instead of a taxguru.in copy; (b) three new RBI entries cite the RBI BE(A)WARE booklet (rbidocs.rbi.org.in, fetched 2026-10-04) for "never share OTP/PIN/passwords" (Part A s.2), screen-sharing apps (s.6) and "use official websites / contact details" (s.1, s.8). Times of India, Times Now, Cashfree and Business Standard no longer appear in the next-steps panel. **Still unresolved, for owner review:** `A_SEBI_CHECK_PAYEE` still cites Rediff (secondary) next to SEBI's SEBI Check page, whose fetched text only reads "check verified UPI ID" and "check account details for safer NEFT/RTGS transfers"; `A_AVOID_GROUPS` still cites TeamLease RegTech (secondary) because SEBI's own PR 27/2025 page was not located; `A_OFFICIAL_CALLBACK` still cites `SANCHAR-FAQ-EXAMPLES`, which was not re-fetched this turn; `A_REPORT_SUSPECT` says "accounts" but the NCRP Report Suspect page lists URLs, numbers, handles and similar (not verified for "accounts"); "Do not pay money ... because of this message" is not stated in those words by SEBI PR 14/2026 or the RBI booklet (both cover credentials, not payment); the RBI booklet says to use the bank's official website and contact details, not literally "the number or app you already have"; RBI's remote-access advice is narrower (download such apps only when advised via the company's official toll-free number, not when contacted from a personal number) than "at a stranger's request"; the NCRP guide is headed "For Delhi Only". Claim-evidence entries (`LEGALBABU-NOGUARANTEE`, `MONEYLIFE-*`, `UNIVEST-b`, `MOF-SEBI-REG-WARN`, `TOI-RBI-*`, `TIMESNOW-*`, `TEAMLEASE-*`) were not audited or changed this turn.
* Several other cited next-step sources are commercial or secondary pages (their tier is displayed; their accuracy was not independently checked). The About page (corrected in this release) now says some sources are official and others are news or commercial pages. The English and Hindi About wording was edited in this release; the Hindi edit is, like all Hindi text, not reviewed by a fluent speaker.
* Of 12 external links on three rendered pages, one official page was loaded, one commercial page was loaded, one returned a 504 on a single attempt, and nine were not checked.
* No endorsement by SEBI, NSDL or the organisers of any text is claimed or verified.

## 6. Registry step
* Some message strings in `saathi_rc/registry/contract.py` (for example "SEBI's website did not answer in time") remain from the earlier package. They cannot be reached in this variant, because nothing contacts SEBI, but they are still covered by the unit tests.
* This variant has no live SEBI lookup at all; the register step is a synthetic demonstration. (The earlier package's live client depended on an undocumented SEBI form without permission, which is why it was removed.)
* A match shows that an entry exists, not who contacted the user. Name matching is conservative (`CONFIRMED` only on identical normalised names).
* The fixture register stops returning results (`SOURCE_STALE`) from the real date 2026-10-10; `SAATHI_TODAY` does not affect this.
* The JSON registry response in fixture mode has no DEMO flag; only the HTML banner marks it. On 2026-10-04 the card headline, text, source line, form heading and button were reworded in the HTML view to say "demo sample list" / "sample data, not SEBI" (English and Hindi; the backend card text is unchanged and still says "SEBI's list", and the JSON API response is not reworded).

## 7. Display and documentation defects found in rc2 (not fixed in this release, except the repeated "If you have already paid..." prefix, fixed 2026-10-04: it now appears once, on the first of the three conditional steps; the next two start "In that case, also")
* The label "(listed below)": on 2026-10-05 the English page started listing the backend's `risk_context` under it (view only, `incident_html` in `saathi_rc/web/views.py`). The Hindi page does not list it (the reasons are English strings and no Hindi text was written), so the label can still point to nothing there. One quote can appear twice.
* English "not enough information" (`CANNOT_ASSESS`) pages open with a plain-language lead added on 2026-10-05 (`CANNOT_ASSESS_EN` in `saathi_rc/web/views.py`); the backend headline and summary are still shown below it. The Hindi page keeps the original text and has no such lead: no Hindi text was written for it.
* An oversized request returns an HTML error page even on the JSON route.
* Docstrings and comments refer to directories (`poc/`, `rc/`, `v0_3/`) and a changelog not included here. Some `eval/` scripts of the development history were left out because they need removed files.

## 8. Operations and process
* Local use only: no TLS, authentication, hosting, monitoring or load testing. Rate limiting keys on the socket address (users behind one proxy share a key). `HOST` defaults to `127.0.0.1`; setting `0.0.0.0` exposes an unauthenticated server.
* No user testing, pilot, independent evaluation or evidence of effect on losses.
* Read-aloud depends on the browser voice and was not tested on real devices; browser checks used headless Chromium on an earlier build (rc1); on 2026-10-04 `tests/browser_final.py` (138 checks, 3 failed, the same 3 as on the baseline: the ordinary-payment over-escalation `I paid 1,200 to Zomato via UPI` shows an urgent block) and `tests/browser_check.py` (no failures) were run on a copy of this candidate.
* **Uncertain-incident page (2026-10-04, display and fixed-text change only):** the "one question first" headline now names the order (bank, then 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, then evidence), and the "if the answer is yes" block shows the conditional bank, 1930 and evidence steps first with "Step 1/2/3" labels and the general do-not-pay step last. The backend step order and the incident classification are unchanged (392-case comparison: 0 differences in posture, incident, steps, claims, indicators, options and questions; only the ASK_FOLLOWUP headline text and the "not checked" wording for a registration number differ). The Hindi wording of these changes is machine-written and not reviewed by a fluent speaker.

## 9. Safety sprint of 2026-10-05
Three narrow changes to Hinglish/Hindi incident reading and a link-plus-consequence rule were added (see `docs/SAFETY_SPRINT_REPORT.md`, which lists them and the gaps they leave). Unchanged known failures: S1 A04, B07, C05, C06, H07 and the three Zomato browser failures. A "SMS se aaye page pe OTP daal diya" report (page, not link) is still not read as an incident, a genuine payment link plus OTP is escalated, and the new consequence wording is English only.

## 10. Adversarial validation of 2026-10-05 (ADV-1 / ADV-2, author-written, not independent)
* Credentials entered on a page that arrived by SMS are still often missed when the text does not use the word "link" or an "SMS ke/se ..." phrase (English: "opened the page it sent", "the site that opened"), or when the marker and the credential are in different comma-separated clauses. A page entry with no explicit marker gets at best a follow-up question.
* The Hinglish/Hindi page-entry rule escalates some ordinary flows: "OTP ka SMS aaya aur maine online shopping site par OTP enter kar diya" (OTP delivered by SMS, then entered on one's own checkout) and "SMS ke link pe maine apne order ka OTP daal diya" (own order, via an SMS link) both produce ESCALATE with urgent steps.
* English and Hindi unauthorised-debit reports are missed when the text lacks the expected money/"my account" phrase (e.g. "My card was used for 12,000 abroad... did not authorise it" is ABSTAIN, "An unknown UPI payment of 4,500 left my account, I did not approve it" is CANNOT_ASSESS); the bank and 1930 steps are listed but not marked urgent.
* Romance/crypto deposit requests ("deposit a little in this crypto app", "daal do", "lagao") are not flagged in 11 of 13 author-written examples (10 CANNOT_ASSESS, 1 ASK_FOLLOWUP); the abstention is not reassuring and the callback, no-pay and bank steps are shown.
* Expiry-type threat messages with a link ("your number will expire in 24 hours, click ...") and Hinglish/Hindi threat wording are not covered by the link-plus-consequence rule.
